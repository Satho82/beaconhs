Baseline: `1cd31832b78053f86e1cf33e2128783e30576f14` (Fast DEV green).
Module: existing `hospitality.compliance`. No catalogue, schema, migration, RLS, workflow, infrastructure or deployment changes.

## Fail-closed contract

The entitlement is required before Compliance RBAC, existing Property/RLS restrictions and resource/action authorization. Missing, disabled, future and expired grants deny access. Platform/super-admin status does not bypass tenant entitlement. Tenant identity comes from the authenticated context or the existing worker transaction, never a submitted tenant ID.

The organisation-wide overview now explicitly requires `compliance.read`; all other existing read/manage/assign permission checks remain. `/compliance/mine` deliberately preserves the existing self-only authorization model: an entitled user may read their own obligations without organisation-wide read permission. The common person read service requires `compliance.read` when the subject is not `ctx.personId`. Existing RLS and Property predicates remain unchanged.

## Shared infrastructure deliberately retained

- Training/certification records, document acknowledgements/reviews, inspection records, equipment checks, form scoring and evidence storage belong to their own modules. They are not disabled simply because the Compliance aggregation module is off.
- Evidence parent locking, target retirement/deletion checks and historical dependency integrity remain active. Disabling a module is not authorization to delete its evidence or break referential integrity.
- The People profile's legacy “Compliance” tab is a summary of training, document acknowledgements, PPE and incidents, not a query of the Compliance obligation engine. Its owning-module permissions and tenant scope are unchanged.
- Property imports and the generic sync/import engines do not import Compliance obligations as a standalone capability. Any subsequent Compliance recalculation reaches the guarded engine. Ordinary importing stays available.
- General Reports/Insights remain usable with authorized non-Compliance sources. Existing definitions may still be listed, but queries and joins to disabled Compliance data fail before execution; stored PDFs are also re-authorized.
- Existing historical notifications remain ordinary inbox records; their destination routes enforce current access. New Compliance to-dos, dispatches and deliveries are suppressed. Already delivered emails or downloaded files cannot be revoked retroactively.
- Cross-module help/designer links can refer to Compliance; they confer no access and lead to the guarded route. Module navigation and operational Compliance tiles are removed when disabled.
- `/api/attachments/[id]` serves evidence capabilities under existing live parent/RLS checks. Document/inspection attachments remain evidence of their owning module, not a licence bypass to the Compliance hub.
